Designing a High-Integrity
U.S. Election Forecasting
System
 
A strong forecast is not a single clever equation. It is a controlled evidence system:
source-vintaged inputs, explicit measurement error, restrained structural priors,
correlated and heavy-tailed uncertainty, joint simulation, and historical-date
validation.
SCOPE
This paper concerns statistical design, data engineering, validation, and governance. It does not produce a live forecast,
assess any current candidate or party, or estimate any current race.

U.S. ELECTION FORECASTING SYSTEM DESIGN
1
Abstract
The strongest defensible U.S. election forecast is a probabilistic, auditable
measurement-and-forecasting system rather than a weighted average of public win probabilities.
Its core should estimate latent voter preference from individual polls; use partial pooling for
sparse states and districts; maintain a small, regularized fundamentals model; separate current
opinion from future opinion movement and Election-Day polling error; and draw complete
elections from a correlated, heavy-tailed joint distribution. The model should be evaluated with
proper scoring rules, calibration, interval coverage, and margin accuracy on entire election cycles
and on historical as-of dates that reproduce the information actually available at the time. These
choices synthesize the dynamic Bayesian tradition associated with Linzer and The Economist, the
empirical poll-adjustment and uncertainty discipline of Silver-era FiveThirtyEight, the 2024
Morris/538 architecture, the ensemble approach documented by Decision Desk HQ, and
VoteHub's complete-cycle validation and effective-poll concepts.
The paper's central recommendation is organizational as much as statistical: build an immutable, versioned evidence
layer before optimizing the model. Every poll correction, economic revision, district boundary, finance report, expert
rating, and candidate-status change must carry both the period it describes and the timestamp at which it became
available. Without that two-time model, apparent backtest accuracy can be created by leakage. With it, competing
specifications can be evaluated honestly and combined only when out-of-sample evidence supports the combination.
The recommended production design is modular. A primary hierarchical Bayesian path should coexist with simpler
challengers; any ensemble weights should be learned from held-out predictive distributions, not chosen because one
public model happened to perform well in the most recent cycle. The system should publish probability distributions,
expected margins, uncertainty intervals, scenario sensitivity, calibration history, and a model-change log while
avoiding false precision.
Research questions
  What exactly should an election forecast estimate, and how should accuracy be defined?
  How should polling, economics, fundamentals, finance, demographics, geography, ratings, and markets enter
 without double-counting information?
 How can cross-race correlation, rare misses, and model uncertainty be represented coherently?
  What validation and data-governance system prevents historical leakage and overfitting?
 Method. The analysis emphasizes official data documentation, original academic research, published methodology, and public source
code. Named forecasting systems are used as design evidence, not as authorities whose final outputs should be averaged.

1. The forecasting object
A polling average asks where measured opinion appears to be now. A forecast asks what distribution of certified
outcomes is implied by everything legitimately known at a particular time. Let Dt denote the full information set
available at time t, and let Y denote the vector of final vote margins. The primary object is therefore a joint predictive
distribution, not a list of independent race calls.
Forecast target:  p(Y | D_t)
Joint draws:  Y^(k) = m_t + error^(k)
The model estimates a distribution over coherent election outcomes; it does not merely label winners.
1.1 Outputs and estimands
The production system should maintain several related but distinct estimands: two-party vote margin; all-candidate
vote share; turnout; Electoral College allocation; and chamber seat totals. The two-party margin is usually the cleanest
core target because it separates the major-party contest from changes in third-party and undecided shares.
All-candidate vote shares and turnout should be modeled in auxiliary layers when they materially affect the translation
to institutional outcomes.
For each race, publish the predictive mean or median margin, central intervals, the probability implied by the draw
distribution, and sensitivity to major assumptions. For the electoral system, publish draw-based distributions rather
than adding marginal probabilities. Maine and Nebraska district allocations, uncontested races, vacancies, runoffs,
candidate replacement, and other institutional rules must be represented explicitly rather than handled by ad hoc
postprocessing.
1.2 The objective: sharpness subject to calibration
Probabilistic forecasts should be as informative as possible while remaining statistically calibrated. Proper scoring
rules reward accurate, honest predictive distributions and penalize both misplaced certainty and unhelpful
vagueness.[1] Accuracy therefore cannot be reduced to the share of favored candidates who won. A system can choose
the more likely side in every race while still assigning probabilities that are too extreme, intervals that under-cover, or
joint seat distributions that are incoherent.
The model-development loss should combine continuous and binary targets: log score or continuous ranked
probability score for margins, Brier or log score for event probabilities, interval coverage and width, and multivariate
scores for joint outcomes. Metrics should be reported by forecast lead time, office, poll density, competitiveness, and
historical era rather than pooled into one flattering headline number.
Design principle. Optimize the complete predictive distribution under historical replay. The simulation count is a
numerical setting; the difficult parameters are the mean vector, uncertainty scales, tails, and covariance structure.

2. What public forecasting systems contribute
The major documented traditions are more complementary than their public disagreements sometimes suggest. The
Economist's 2020 repository exposes a dynamic multilevel Bayesian implementation in R and Stan. It adds poll-mode
and population corrections, informative state priors, and empirically constructed state correlations to the Linzer
framework.[3][4][5] Silver-era FiveThirtyEight emphasizes empirical poll weighting, house effects, prolific-pollster

2.1 The recommended synthesis
The strongest architecture uses The Economist/Linzer style measurement as the primary statistical spine, Silver-style
empirical poll diagnostics, Morris-style separation of drift and terminal error, and VoteHub/DDHQ-style challengers
and complete-cycle validation. The final forecast should be generated from one controlled data system. Public forecast
probabilities should be retained only as external benchmarks unless their underlying information sets and historical
vintages are known.
This recommendation deliberately separates a generative core from an ensemble layer. The core states how polls and
fundamentals produce a joint election-day distribution. The ensemble layer may combine genuinely distinct predictive
distributions, but only with weights trained on out-of-sample historical forecasts. Predictive stacking is appropriate
because it chooses weights for forecast performance rather than assuming one candidate model is literally true.[13]

3. Data collection: build the evidence system first
Every forecasting record needs two clocks. event_time says when the underlying fact occurred or what period it
describes. available_at says when a historical forecaster could first have known it. A poll may finish fieldwork on one
date and be published later; an economic estimate may describe an earlier quarter but be released and revised on still
later dates; a campaign-finance report covers a defined period but appears after filing and processing. Historical replay
must filter on available_at, never merely on event date.
eligible_record(r, t) = 1  only if  r.available_at <= t
historical_query(t) = latest version known by t, not latest version known today
 The same rule applies to polls, economics, ratings, filings, boundaries, candidates, and model metadata.

3.3 Poll discovery and reconciliation
A production collector should ingest individual polls rather than another site's average. Start from original releases
and archive a permissible copy or structured extract. Use external poll lists to discover missing items, then reconcile by
pollster, sponsor, geography, field dates, sample population, mode, candidate set, and questionnaire. The AAPOR
Transparency Initiative provides a strong metadata baseline: sponsorship and funding, instrument wording, target
population, recruitment and sample construction, mode, dates, sample size, weighting, and precision disclosures.[2]
The collector should preserve adults, registered-voter, and likely-voter versions separately; two-way and multiway
questions separately; and each release/correction separately. When a poll reports multiple candidate matchups or
populations, treat them as correlated measurements from one underlying study, not independent polls.
4. Polling as a measurement model
Let thetas,t be latent two-party preference in geography s on day t. A poll is a noisy observation of that state, shifted by
organization, method, population, sponsor, questionnaire, and other study-level effects. This framing follows the
dynamic Bayesian literature and the public Economist implementation.[3][4][5]
poll_margin_i = theta[s(i), t(i)] + house[j(i)] + mode[k(i)] + population[q(i)]
                 + sponsor_i + questionnaire_i + sampling_error_i + extra_error_i
All adjustments are hierarchically regularized and estimated only from data available inside the training fold.
4.1 Likelihood and overdispersion
A binomial likelihood is a useful starting point for candidate counts, but real polls are not simple random samples.
Weighting, clustering, nonresponse, turnout screens, panel reuse, and design choices introduce extra variation. Polling
error studies find meaningful election-level bias and excess variance beyond nominal sampling error.[14][25] A robust
implementation can use a Student-t margin likelihood or a binomial/beta-binomial formulation with design-effect and
extra-error terms. The tails and scale should be estimated by historical cycle, office, lead time, and poll type, with
partial pooling where samples are thin.
4.2 House effects, bias, and reliability are different
Estimate a pollster's systematic lean and its residual variance separately. A firm can be consistently shifted but precise
after correction; another can be unbiased on average but noisy. Both parameters require hierarchical shrinkage. New
or low-volume pollsters should begin near the industry distribution and move only as evidence accumulates.
house_j ~ Normal(0, tau_house)
log(extra_sd_j) ~ Normal(mu_quality, tau_quality)
The posterior moves away from the population mean only when the pollster's history is informative.

4.3 Preventing pollster flooding and pseudo-replication
Tracking polls, repeated releases, shared samples, and many polls from one organization do not create independent
information. Silver-era models explicitly cap prolific pollsters, and VoteHub first compresses releases into
pollster-level signals before computing an effective number of polls.[7][12] The recommended model should represent a
study-series cluster and estimate within-series correlation. A public ENOP diagnostic can be computed from
normalized influence weights, but the internal likelihood should do the deeper work of identifying shared samples and
organizations.
ENOP = (sum_i w_i)^2 / sum_i(w_i^2)
A descriptive information measure, not a substitute for a correctly clustered likelihood.
4.4 Time dynamics and borrowing strength
Latent opinion should move smoothly but not rigidly. A correlated random walk, local linear trend, spline, or Gaussian
process can connect adjacent days. The innovation covariance should permit national movement, regional or
demographic movement, and local deviations. Sparse states and districts then borrow from national polling, similar
geographies, and structural priors without being treated as clones.
theta_t = theta_(t-1) + L_time * z_t,     z_t ~ robust multivariate noise
 theta[s,t] = national_t + state_baseline_s + local_deviation[s,t]
 L_time controls which geographies tend to move together during the campaign.
Mode, population, and questionnaire effects should vary by era when evidence supports it. Historical landline effects
need not equal modern web-panel effects. A hierarchical era interaction is safer than a permanent universal
adjustment, and every added interaction must clear complete-cycle validation.
5. Fundamentals: a restrained structural prior
Fundamentals are valuable when race-specific polling is sparse and early campaign polls have substantial time to
move. They are also unusually vulnerable to overfitting. Presidential models have only a small number of modern
cycles, while the analyst can choose among many plausible economic definitions, windows, transformations, and
interactions. Traditional structural models show why growth, approval, incumbency, and party tenure are plausible
predictors, but their specifications differ and should be treated as candidate hypotheses rather than settled
law.[26][27][28]
5.1 Presidential prior
Use a small set of predeclared, historically available predictors: prior national and state two-party vote; state relative
lean; incumbency and whether the incumbent is personally running; party tenure; presidential approval; recent real
income or output growth; inflation or real-income pressure; and a limited polarization interaction. Morris/538 used
regularized Bayesian regression, and The Economist anchored its Election-Day prior to a regularized fundamentals
forecast.[4][8]
fundamental_margin_s = beta0 + beta1*prior_lean_s + beta2*approval
                     + beta3*real_income_growth + beta4*incumbency + beta5*party_tenure + u_s
 Illustrative specification. Coefficients and predictor windows must be selected in nested complete-cycle validation.

5.2 Congressional and gubernatorial priors
The larger number of House, Senate, and gubernatorial contests permits more structure, but observations within a
cycle share the same national environment and are not independent. Useful predictors include district or state lean,
the generic ballot, recent office-specific performance, incumbency/open-seat status, candidate experience,
fundraising, special-election evidence, and statewide environment. Silver-era 538, Morris/538, DDHQ, and VoteHub
all document variants of these ingredients.[7][9][10][11][12]
Transform finance variables to reflect scale and matched reporting windows. A log ratio or share of candidate receipts
is more stable than a raw dollar difference; cash on hand, spending, independent expenditures, debts, and small-donor
receipts should remain distinct. Because fundraising partly reflects donor beliefs about candidate quality and
competitiveness, it is predictive but not automatically causal. The FEC also warns that summary files can double-count
transfers among authorized committees, which should be netted or modeled carefully.[18]
5.3 Economic vintage discipline
The economic feature store should be reconstructed from real-time releases. ALFRED explicitly distinguishes the
observation date from the real-time period during which a value was known and provides vintage-date queries.[15] For
every backtest run, use the latest value available on that date, not the latest revised value now. When an Election-Day
economic quantity was not yet released, either carry forward the last known release or include a separate nowcast
distribution; do not silently substitute later data.
Predeclare transformation windows - for example, year-over-year real disposable income growth or cumulative recent
growth - and select among them within nested cycle-level validation. Store seasonal-adjustment flags, unit
transformations, release lag, and revision history. Missing historical coverage should be imputed inside the training
fold only, with imputation uncertainty propagated.
5.4 Regularization and model class
Begin with hierarchical ridge or regularized Bayesian regression. Add Elastic Net, shallow trees, boosting, or small
neural networks only as challengers. DDHQ and VoteHub document ensembles of linear and nonlinear learners;
VoteHub also performs year-aware tuning inside each held-out-year fold.[11][12] That nested structure is the minimum
protection against a high-dimensional model learning the test cycle through preprocessing or tuning.
Admission rule. A variable enters production only if its definition is stable, its historical availability can be reconstructed,
its missingness is understood, and it improves complete-cycle predictive performance or calibration without creating
fragile tail behavior.
6. Demographics, district geography, and redistricting
Geography is not a static lookup. Congressional and legislative districts change; precincts split and merge; county
equivalents change; and ACS estimates have their own vintages and sampling error. The data model must separate a
stable place identity from the boundary polygon and the election in which that boundary applies. Census redistricting
products, PL 94-171 data, APIs, and TIGER/Line provide official demographic and spatial building blocks.[22][23]

6.2 Feature construction
Build district demographics from contemporaneous census units using population-weighted or
voting-age-population-weighted crosswalks. Keep citizen voting-age population, total population, age, race/ethnicity,
education, income, density, urbanicity, industry, and migration measures as separate features with margins of error.

For redistricted districts, recalculate historical partisan baselines on the new boundary using precinct or block-level

A similarity matrix may combine geographic proximity, recent partisan lean, density, education, race/ethnicity, media
market, and economic structure. Estimate the mapping from similarity to residual correlation only within training
cycles. A factor model or Gaussian-process kernel guarantees a positive-semidefinite covariance matrix more reliably
than editing pairwise correlations by hand.

7.1 Polling path and fundamentals prior
The primary model should be a hierarchical state-space model whose Election-Day latent state is weakly anchored by a
structural prior. The Economist implementation runs the opinion path backward from an Election-Day fundamentals
prior so that polls update that prior coherently.[4][5] A forward-filtering formulation is also valid if it estimates current
opinion and explicitly projects future movement to Election Day. The choice between forward and backward
parameterization is computational; the validation target is the same predictive distribution.
Do not add fundamentals twice. If fundamentals define the Bayesian prior inside the polling model, a second additive
fundamentals correction at the end duplicates evidence unless the entire combined specification is backtested as such.
If separate poll-only and fundamentals-only distributions are retained for stacking, the primary model's prior should
be broad enough that the components remain meaningfully distinct.
7.2 Separate three uncertainties
final_margin_s - current_latent_margin[s,t]
    = future_opinion_movement[s,t] + Election_Day_polling_error[s] + turnout_or_translation_error[s]
 Parameter and model uncertainty sit around every component and must also be integrated.
Future opinion movement should shrink as Election Day approaches. Election-Day polling error should not.
Morris/538 explicitly estimated historical poll movement by campaign date separately from terminal polling bias, and
Silver-era methodology likewise distinguishes campaign movement from final error.[6][8] This separation prevents a
forecast from becoming mechanically certain merely because the calendar reached Election Day.

7.3 Multi-candidate and turnout extensions
The simplest core can model the two-party margin and treat undecided/other share as an auxiliary outcome. A fuller
specification can use a logistic-normal composition for major candidates, other candidates, and undecided
respondents, with questionnaire-specific mapping. Turnout should usually be a separate, uncertainty-bearing model
informed by historical participation, registration and eligible-population data, not an unobserved deterministic
correction to vote preference. Translating voter intention into votes may require an additional turnout-screen error
term.
7.4 Inference and diagnostics
Fit the core with Stan, PyMC, or another system that exposes posterior predictive checks and diagnostics. Require
stable chains or reliable approximate-inference diagnostics, high effective sample sizes for decision-relevant
quantities, no material divergences, and simulation-based calibration on synthetic data. Run prior predictive checks
before fitting historical elections. The public Economist model demonstrates the value of inspectable code and also
documents how external scrutiny exposed questionable predictions, bugs, and modeling choices.[4][5]
8. Correlated and fat-tailed uncertainty
Cross-race dependence is indispensable. If the polling industry misestimates the composition of the electorate or a
national environment measure is biased, many states or districts can miss in the same direction. Shirani-Mehr and
colleagues decompose polling misses into election-level bias and poll-level variation, while public forecasters model
national, regional/demographic, and local components.[14][7][8][11][12]
error_r = loading_national[r]*G + loading_region[r]*R_region(r)
         + feature_loadings[r]'*F + local_scale[r]*u_r
G, R, F, and u may share a scale-mixture shock to produce heavy tails.

8.1 Factor covariance
Use a low-rank factor structure plus race-specific residual variance. National factors capture common miss; region or
demographic factors capture structured deviations; local residuals capture candidate and survey idiosyncrasies. The
factor loadings can be regularized functions of geography, office, poll density, and demographics. This structure scales
to hundreds of House races, remains positive-semidefinite, and yields interpretable stress scenarios.
8.2 Heavy tails and surprise regimes
Normal errors understate the chance of large joint misses when historical samples are small and data-generating
processes change. Morris/538 documented a Student-t error layer; DDHQ describes heavy-tailed priors in part of its
ensemble.[8][11] A robust simulator can draw from a multivariate Student-t distribution or a scale mixture in which a
cycle-level shock inflates all error components together. Degrees of freedom and scale should be regularized and tested
by held-out predictive scores and interval coverage, not chosen to create a desired level of suspense.
8.3 Nonstationarity
Polling methodology, polarization, turnout patterns, redistricting, and media environments change. Estimate era
effects and allow recent cycles more influence, but do not simply discard older elections: they contain rare-error
information that newer samples lack. A hierarchical time-varying scale, robust mixture, or discounted likelihood can
balance relevance and tail estimation. Report sensitivity to reasonable era windows.

9.2 What to ensemble
Maintain structurally different challengers: a hierarchical Bayesian core; a robust poll-only state-space model; a

9.3 What not to ensemble
Do not average published probabilities from The Economist, 538, Silver Bulletin, DDHQ, and VoteHub as if they were
independent observations. Those products often ingest overlapping polls, economic releases, ratings, and market
information; they may target different estimands; and historical revisions may not be reproducible. An average can
conceal, rather than reduce, common-mode error. Use public forecasts as external benchmarks or as a separately
labeled consensus model only when versioned archives and a transparent validation design exist.[29]
9.4 Optional expert and market layers
Expert ratings and prediction markets can contain information not easily encoded in structured data, especially in
sparsely polled races. They also echo polls, fundraising, news, and each other. Enter them as separate timestamped
modules with explicit transformations, liquidity or category uncertainty, and strict ablation. VoteHub and Morris/538
document two different ways of integrating those signals.[10][12] The production report should always show results
with and without optional layers.
10. Validation, backtesting, and leakage prevention
Random race-level train/test splits are invalid for the primary performance claim because races within an election year
share national conditions, polling errors, candidate environments, and data-production choices. Hold out complete
cycles. For each held-out cycle, replay multiple forecast dates, reconstruct the as-of data snapshot, tune models only
within the remaining cycles, and generate the full joint distribution. VoteHub documents this year-held-out and
nested-tuning principle explicitly.[12]

10.3 Baselines and ablations
Every complex model should face simple baselines: last-election lean plus national swing; equal-weight recent polls; a
shrinkage polling average; a parsimonious fundamentals regression; and independent-error simulation. Then remove
one component at a time: pollster quality, house effects, sponsor effect, fundamentals, ratings, markets, regional
factors, heavy tails, and recent-cycle weighting. A component earns production status only if its gain is stable across
multiple held-out cycles or materially improves a known risk such as interval under-coverage.

  Object storage for immutable raw artifacts; relational or columnar tables for normalized records; a spatial database

11.3 Monitoring
Operational alerts should cover stale sources, failed poll parsing, suspicious duplicate IDs, large corrections, missing
geographic mappings, FEC amendments, candidate-status changes, posterior diagnostic failures, unstable
probabilities across seeds, and implausibly abrupt model moves. Distinguish a data update from a code update and a
parameter update in the public change log.
Each release should have a signed or hashed manifest. Preserve old public forecasts exactly as published. If an error is
corrected, publish both the original and corrected artifact, the first affected timestamp, the statistical impact, and the
prevention test added afterward. The Economist experience demonstrates that public code and active model checking
can surface errors that internal development missed.[4]

12. Governance and communication
Election forecasts influence public interpretation, journalism, campaigns, and markets. The modeling team should
separate research, production, editorial presentation, and approval roles. A model-change committee should review
any current-cycle overlay, discretionary adjustment, new source, or recalibration against a written standard. Manual
race adjustments should be exceptional, timestamped, reproducible, and accompanied by an unadjusted output.
12.1 Model card
  Forecast target, population, unit, election rules, update cadence, and earliest supported date.
  Complete source inventory with licensing, vintages, coverage, exclusions, and known gaps.
  Mathematical specification, priors, covariance factors, tail distribution, and ensemble components.
  Training elections, held-out design, historical as-of grid, preprocessing boundaries, and all metrics.
  Calibration and interval coverage by lead time, office, poll density, and historical era.
  Known limitations: small presidential sample, changing polling methods, redistricting, missing polls, data
 revisions, and unknown unknowns.
12.2 Public presentation
Lead with the expected margin range and major uncertainty drivers before displaying a precise win probability. Round
displayed probabilities and intervals to a precision justified by Monte Carlo stability and historical calibration. Show
scenario sensitivity - for example, a generic national polling miss, a regional miss, or reduced poll quality - without
treating those scenarios as new forecasts. Explain that correlated uncertainty allows many races to move together.
Publish the forecast archive, methodology, data dictionary, change log, and validation report. Open source the
executable core where licensing permits. When raw polls or ratings cannot be redistributed, publish hashes, source
metadata, transformation code, and synthetic fixtures so the pipeline can still be audited.
12.3 Red-team tests
  Pollster flood: add many overlapping releases from one firm and verify that information grows sublinearly.
  Common polling miss: shift all polls coherently and test national/regional propagation.
  Heavy-tail shock: inflate the cycle scale and verify plausible joint tail behavior.
  Candidate replacement or withdrawal: confirm that data lineage, eligibility rules, and publication state change
 cleanly.
 Redistricting: swap boundary vintages and require hard failure rather than a silent crosswalk.
  Source outage: reproduce the last forecast exactly and label stale inputs instead of partially updating.
  Leakage canary: inject a future-dated record and require the as-of builder and test suite to reject it.
 
13. Conclusion
The strongest possible U.S. election forecast is not the model with the most variables, the newest
algorithm, or the largest simulation count. It is the system that most faithfully represents the
information available at the time, distinguishes measurement from future change, preserves
dependence across races, remains humble about rare errors, and proves each design choice on
unseen election cycles.

A dynamic hierarchical polling model should form the primary spine. A restrained, regularized fundamentals model
should supply early and sparse-race structure. Correlated Student-t or scale-mixture errors should preserve national,
regional, demographic, and local miss mechanisms. Joint simulation should translate margins into institutional
outcomes. Predictive stacking may combine genuinely different models, but only with fold-pure historical predictions.
Ratings and markets should be optional, timestamped modules whose incremental value is visible through ablation.
The decisive advantage, however, is the evidence architecture: immutable raw artifacts, bitemporal records,
boundary-aware geography, economic vintages, matched finance periods, model manifests, and historical-date replay.
That infrastructure turns methodological disagreement into testable hypotheses and protects backtests from
hindsight. The result is not certainty. It is a forecast whose uncertainty is earned, whose history can be reconstructed,
and whose failures can teach the next version.
Minimum viable high-integrity model: source-vintaged polling and results; a hierarchical latent-opinion path; a
parsimonious structural prior; complete-cycle historical replay; national/regional/local heavy-tailed errors; joint
simulation; proper scores and calibration; immutable forecast archives. Add complexity only after these foundations work.
