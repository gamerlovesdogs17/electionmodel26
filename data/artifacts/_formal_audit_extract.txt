1. Executive verdict
ElectionModel26 is a strong research-engineering prototype, but it is not yet a trustworthy
public election forecast. The architecture is thoughtful, modular, and unusually transparent. The
current empirical foundation is not capable of validating it.
 69
 automated tests passed
 A-
 architecture grade
 F
 validation grade
 3/10
 production readiness
RELEASE DECISION
Do not publish or promote the current probabilities as validated forecasts. Preserve the engineering shell, rebuild the
historical evidence base, and regenerate every score, calibration chart, ensemble weight, and release gate from official
race and result data.
Why the verdict is severe
The historical race universe is generated from approximate fixture logic rather than the actual Senate ballot for each
cycle. Synthetic races are then joined to a partial results archive and treated as realized history. In 2022 this process
constructs the wrong contested seats and the wrong chamber outcome. A backtest can be perfectly leak-free and still be
invalid when it replays the wrong election.
Why the project is worth repairing
The system already contains many of the difficult structural elements: bitemporal evidence records, pollster concentration
controls, effective-number-of-polls diagnostics, hierarchical models, heavy-tail concepts, joint seat simulation, release
manifests, model cards, signing, scenario tooling, and an extensive test suite. The largest required change is not a
wholesale rewrite. It is an evidence and validation reset, followed by statistical re-estimation.
Audit conclusion in one sentence
 The codebase demonstrates forecasting maturity; the packaged validation results do not.

5. Critical finding 1 - historical truth is invalid
SEVERITY: CRITICAL
The current race-level and chamber-level validation metrics are not evidence of real-world forecast accuracy.
What the code does
The cycle fixture code constructs a recurring set of states from a seat-class approximation and pads presidential years
with randomly chosen complements. It then maps races to a simplified seat representation and generates synthetic truths
and polls. The source itself identifies these records as synthetic research fixtures and states that they are not for
publication as real forecasts.

Observed 2022 failure
The normalized 2022 data contain a Class-II-like set of contested states rather than the actual Class III ballot plus special
elections. Within that constructed universe, the validation path counts 31 Democratic/independent held seats and 12
Democratic-winning rows, yielding approximately 43 seats and no Democratic control. The actual election retained
Democratic Senate control. Because the model also assigned no control in the packaged artifact, the reported chamber
Brier score of zero rewards agreement with an incorrect truth.
Required control
Each cycle must begin with an independently sourced, versioned ballot table. Before scoring, the pipeline must reconcile
all continuing seats, regular contests, special contests, runoffs, party changes, and the resulting 100-seat chamber
against official certified results. Any mismatch must stop validation.

7. Finding 3 - the core is hierarchical, not dynamic
SEVERITY: HIGH
The production PyMC model should either implement a date-indexed latent opinion path or be described as a static
hierarchical Election-Day measurement model.
Current formulation
In midterms/model/pymc_model.py:172-305, each race receives an Election-Day latent margin and a future component.
Polls, regardless of field date, effectively observe the same current race state after that component is removed. Date
enters mainly through externally computed recency weights rather than through a stochastic opinion path.

Why the distinction matters
A recency-weighted static state can summarize current evidence, but it cannot estimate the trajectory of opinion,
distinguish genuine movement from irregular polling density, or correctly propagate early-campaign uncertainty. The
challenger state-space code is closer to the target, although its process variance should depend on elapsed time rather
than on the number of poll observations.
Recommended specification
Use a dynamic national path plus partially pooled race deviations. Separate measurement error from process movement.
Estimate or hierarchically shrink the path volatility by campaign stage, then introduce Election-Day error as a distinct
terminal draw. Validate the dynamic model against a static baseline at every historical lead time.

8. Finding 4 - correlation and tail risk are incomplete
SEVERITY: HIGH
The model contains the right vocabulary of uncertainty, but the production covariance structure and its scales have not
earned their place through valid historical testing.
What is strong
 - Joint simulation is performed across the chamber rather than treating races as independent.
 - National and regional factors create coherent movement.
 - Heavy-tailed distributions and common shocks reduce the danger of overconfident Gaussian tails.
 - Similarity-based demographic and geographic covariance exists in the codebase.
 
What is missing or inconsistent
 - The production PyMC terminal bias is primarily a single common scalar rather than national plus race/state and
 demographic-similarity terminal layers.
- The richer similarity covariance is used by challenger implementations but is not consistently part of the production
 core.
- Key uncertainty scales are substantially hand-set.
 - Packaged 2022 ablations show improved CRPS when heavy tails or correlated errors are removed. Because that
 replay is invalid, the result cannot justify either retaining or removing those components.

9. Finding 5 - fundamentals and provenance
SEVERITY: HIGH

10. Finding 6 - ensemble and validation claims
SEVERITY: CRITICAL
The packaged ensemble is a CRPS-weighted mixture, not independently validated predictive stacking. Its production
weights cannot be relied upon.
Current mechanism
midterms/model/ensemble.py converts mean component CRPS values into softmax weights and samples an entire
component distribution for each joint draw. This is a legitimate mixture design, but it does not optimize the held-out
predictive density in the way the term stacking ordinarily implies.
Provenance failure
The packaged stack provenance covers only 2018, 2020, and 2022 and states that mass assigned to the fast hierarchical
implementation was remapped onto PyMC. A model must receive only the weight earned by its own out-of-fold forecasts.
If PyMC fails or is too expensive to run, the report must say that it was not scored; it must not inherit another
implementation's score.
Additional validation defects
 - The latest validation report is marked quick and uses only 150 draws at lead-time checkpoints, which is insufficient for
 stable tail or chamber probabilities.
- The primary calibration summary contains only 33 constructed 2022 races, with many probability bins containing one to
 three observations.
- The expert-rating ablation uses a model-derived synthetic rating shift rather than a true historical ratings archive.
 - The peer gate relies partly on hard-coded approximate probabilities rather than immutable primary-source snapshots.
 - A validation path can silently fall back from PyMC to the fast model while retaining the PyMC label.
 
Correct ensemble protocol
Generate predictions from every component inside each outer historical-cycle holdout. Select hyperparameters and
ensemble weights only from inner training cycles. Optimize a proper score such as log score or CRPS under nonnegative
simplex constraints, or use regularized out-of-fold meta-learning. Preserve component identity and record failures
explicitly.

Positive operational controls
The release design is materially better than many research prototypes. It includes model cards, a changelog, correction

are complete. Future tests should include official-history reconciliation and golden files derived independently of the
implementation.

Recommended score suite
Use race-level log score, Brier score, CRPS, interval coverage, calibration slope/intercept, and probability integral
transform diagnostics. At the chamber level use control Brier/log score, seat-distribution CRPS or ranked probability
score, tail coverage, and multivariate energy or variogram scores. Always report sample sizes, uncertainty across cycles,
and numerical Monte Carlo error.
Pre-registration discipline
Before each outer holdout or prospective forecast, freeze the feature definitions, eligible sources, hyperparameter search,
scoring rule, tie handling, and correction policy. This prevents selecting the validation story after observing the election.

Stop conditions
 - Do not tune the model to compensate for unresolved race or result mismatches.
 - Do not publish a chamber-control probability from a run that contains fixture evidence.
 - Do not describe an ensemble as stacked unless every weight derives from model-specific out-of-fold predictions.
 - Do not accept a passing aggregate score if a simpler baseline wins or calibration materially fails.
 
First publishable milestone
RECOMMENDED MILESTONE
A frozen shadow forecast using an official 2014-2024 historical archive, reproducible nested holdouts, a clearly
identified model spine, and no fixture inputs. Public-facing live probabilities should follow, not precede, this milestone.

Residual-risk principle
Election forecasting cannot eliminate uncertainty. The goal is to represent it honestly. Residual risk becomes acceptable
only when data lineage is explicit, historical scoring is valid, uncertainty is calibrated, and the published artifact clearly
states what the model does not know.

Methodological note on grades
Letter grades are audit judgments, not posterior estimates. They summarize the supplied implementation against the
blueprint and published peer practices. Production readiness is deliberately constrained by the weakest validity-critical
layer; it is not the arithmetic mean of the component grades.
Final recommendation
PROCEED WITH A CONTROLLED REBUILD
Retain the warehouse concepts, poll controls, joint simulator, governance artifacts, and challenger framework. Replace
the historical truth and poll archives, make evidence eligibility fail closed, re-estimate the model, and require a clean
nested validation report before any public forecast release.
